# Photosynthetic and Semi-Artificial Biohybrid Energy Conversion

**Classification:** H/B; active laboratory research, with direct biophotovoltaics remaining a low-power technology relative to conventional PV

## Research objective
Compare electricity generation, solar-to-chemical conversion and biologically assisted catalysis using an equal incident-energy boundary.

## Biological principle
Photosystems capture photons and drive redox chemistry. Semi-artificial systems combine engineered light absorbers or semiconductors with enzymes or microbes, pairing materials-based photon capture with biological catalytic selectivity.

## Engineering scope and system boundary
Treat whole-cell biophotovoltaics, semiconductor–microbe/enzyme hybrids and artificial-leaf photoelectrochemical chemical production as separate device classes. Record absorbed photon flux, spectral response, charge separation, electron-transfer efficiency, selectivity, catalyst lifetime and all parasitic loads.

## Mathematical formulation

For incident spectral irradiance I_lambda and aperture area A, define incident power using the projected geometry. Define solar-to-chemical efficiency eta_STC=product energy rate / incident optical power. For electrical devices use eta_el=P_el/P_in. Do not compare endpoints without accounting for storage, purification and product quality. Close the electron balance.

## Parameters requiring evidence
Spectral irradiance, active area, optical absorption, charge-separation yield, electron-transfer resistance, current density, Faradaic efficiency, product rate, selectivity, catalyst lifetime, reactor pressure, illumination geometry and auxiliary power.

## First validation protocol
Reconstruct energy and electron balances from published device measurements. Compare electricity, chemical production and hybrid architectures under the same photon flux, active area, product basis and durability period. Include a conventional PV-plus-electrolyzer or PV-plus-chemical-process baseline.

## Falsification criteria
Reject a biohybrid advantage if it disappears after parasitic power, purification, catalyst degradation, material replacement and area-normalized throughput are counted.

## Limitations and safety boundary
Do not present biological photovoltaics as Dyson-swarm-grade electrical collectors. Their potential value may be solar-to-chemical manufacturing even where electrical power density is uncompetitive.

## Initial source direction
Qi et al. 2024 whole-cell biophotovoltaics review; Yehezkeli 2026 Chemical Reviews; 2026 Nature Sustainability perspective on practical photosynthetic biohybrids.
