# Mycelium-Derived Composites and Insulation

**Classification:** B/H; terrestrial composites are demonstrated; space-habitat use is proposed, not qualified

## Research objective
Map the process–structure–property envelope of mycelium composites and determine appropriate noncritical space-habitat functions.

## Biological principle
Fungal hyphae form interwoven networks that bind lignocellulosic particles. Growth morphology, substrate packing, fungal species, moisture and post-processing affect density, stiffness, strength and failure mode.

## Engineering scope and system boundary
Consider packaging, interior panels, acoustic damping, replaceable insulation and non-load-bearing secondary components. Exclude primary pressure hulls and radiation shields unless separately demonstrated. Evaluate dehydration, flame behaviour, containment, vacuum compatibility and outgassing.

## Mathematical formulation

Record density rho, porosity phi, hyphal orientation tensor Q, particle distribution and moisture. Fit material-specific constitutive laws sigma=F(strain;rho,phi,Q,moisture,species,process). Do not impose a universal power law without evidence. Add thermal conduction and moisture-coupled degradation models.

## Parameters requiring evidence
Fungal species, substrate composition, growth time, temperature, humidity, compaction, post-treatment, density, porosity, orientation, tensile/compressive strength, modulus, thermal conductivity, moisture sensitivity, outgassing and ageing.

## First validation protocol
Reproduce published structure–property data where accessible. Use standardized coupons and matched nonliving insulation baselines. Test tensile/compression, cyclic loading, thermal conductivity, humidity cycling, flame and vacuum compatibility. Report batch variability.

## Falsification criteria
Reject a target application if performance, outgassing, flammability, microbial stability or durability fails its specification, or if protective encapsulation erases the mass/lifecycle advantage.

## Limitations and safety boundary
Mycelium composites may offer useful thermal or packaging properties, but properties vary with process and structure. Terrestrial material performance is not space qualification.
