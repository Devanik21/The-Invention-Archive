# Regolith Biomineralization and Non-Primary Construction Materials

**Classification:** B/H; terrestrial MICP is demonstrated, while extraterrestrial construction remains low-maturity research

## Research objective
Quantify whether biomineralization can make useful non-primary construction products from characterized lunar or Martian regolith simulants.

## Biological principle
Microbial ureolysis or other biological routes can raise carbonate saturation and precipitate mineral binders between particles. Coral, bone and shells are inspirations, but engineering performance depends on organism, ions, pH and feedstocks.

## Engineering scope and system boundary
Limit initial uses to pavers, berms, dust stabilization, repair fills and non-pressure-bearing panels. Do not assign pressure-hull or radiation-shielding roles without independent qualification. Account for water, calcium, carbon, nitrogen, urea or other feedstocks, and residual byproducts.

## Mathematical formulation

Define saturation ratio Omega=IAP/Ksp; supersaturation is necessary but not sufficient for useful deposition. Model deposition rate dot(M_c)=k_p A_s f(Omega,pH,T,X). Couple porosity and mineral distribution to measured constitutive and fracture properties. Track reactants, side products and imported feedstocks.

## Parameters requiring evidence
Simulant composition, calcium availability, carbonate supply, pH, temperature, microbial kinetics, liquid distribution, low-gravity regime, porosity, precipitation uniformity, compressive strength, fracture toughness and thermal-cycle damage.

## First validation protocol
Use matched regolith simulants and non-biological binder baselines. Characterize mineral phase and measure compressive strength, permeability, abrasion, thermal cycling and fracture. Include water and reagent budgets.

## Falsification criteria
Reject a construction claim if strength/durability is inadequate for its declared use, if required reagents/water outweigh the abiotic baseline, or if byproducts cannot be contained and removed.

## Limitations and safety boundary
MICP-based extraterrestrial construction is a proposal, not a flight-qualified technology. Low gravity changes fluid and particle behaviour; Earth-simulant success does not prove lunar or Martian performance.
