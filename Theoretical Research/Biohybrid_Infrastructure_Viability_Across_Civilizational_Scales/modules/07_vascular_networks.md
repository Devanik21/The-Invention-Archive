# Vascular-Inspired Fluid, Nutrient and Thermal Networks

**Classification:** M; biomimetic network design with conventional fluid-engineering baselines

## Research objective
Test whether branching transport geometries inspired by blood vessels, xylem and leaf venation reduce pumping, material mass or failure propagation.

## Biological principle
Biological vascular systems balance transport demand, branching geometry, growth and redundancy. Murray's law is an idealized result under specific assumptions, not a universal scaling law.

## Engineering scope and system boundary
Consider coolant loops, nutrient distribution, gas manifolds and fluid networks in photobioreactors and modular habitats. Distinguish pumped single-phase flow from two-phase or capillary systems in microgravity.

## Mathematical formulation

For a cylindrical laminar channel, Delta p=8 mu L Q/(pi r^4) under Hagen–Poiseuille assumptions. An idealized Murray relation r_0^3=sum_k r_k^3 follows only under its cost assumptions. Optimize topology and radius with constraints on pressure, flow, shear, mass, leakage and isolation. Report a Pareto set or disclose scalar weights.

## Parameters requiring evidence
Viscosity, density, flow demand, channel length/radius, roughness, pump efficiency, shear limits, pressure, leakage, fluid phase, gravity regime, maintenance access and redundancy.

## First validation protocol
Compare branching and conventional manifolds with identical flow, pressure and boundaries. Simulate blockage, leakage, pump loss and control failure. Verify predictions with CFD or bench measurements.

## Falsification criteria
Reject the design if it improves no declared objective without unacceptable degradation in another, or if its apparent benefit comes from mismatched constraints.

## Limitations and safety boundary
Biological branching geometry is an inspiration, not evidence that a particular scaling rule is optimal for every network. Microgravity changes fluid distribution.

## Initial source direction
Hagen–Poiseuille flow and Murray's vascular minimization principle; add primary fluid-mechanics sources during literature audit.

**Status:** Proposed module. No experiment or numerical validation is claimed here.
