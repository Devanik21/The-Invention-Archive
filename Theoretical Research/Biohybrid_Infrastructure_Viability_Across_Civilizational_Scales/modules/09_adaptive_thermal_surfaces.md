# Biomimetic Adaptive Thermal and Radiative Surfaces

**Classification:** M; material/optical design proposal, with radiation as the controlling heat-rejection mechanism in vacuum

## Research objective
Assess biological-surface-inspired optical and emissive structures for thermal regulation in collectors, spacecraft and habitat surfaces.

## Biological principle
Insect cuticles, leaf surfaces and reflective scales motivate hierarchical optical textures. The engineering system is nonliving; its performance comes from measured spectral absorptance, reflectance, emissivity and durability.

## Engineering scope and system boundary
Model terrestrial passive cooling separately from spacecraft radiators. In vacuum there is no convective cooling to space; waste heat must be radiated. Do not apply transpiration analogies unless working fluid and mass loss are explicitly budgeted.

## Mathematical formulation

For a grey approximation use Q_rad=epsilon sigma A(T^4-T_bg^4). A detailed model integrates spectral and directional emissivity against the Planck distribution and geometry/view factors. Solar absorptance and infrared emissivity are separate properties.

## Parameters requiring evidence
Spectral absorptance, hemispherical emissivity, radiator area, view factors, solar incidence, background radiation, temperature range, contamination, erosion, degradation, coating mass and thermal cycling.

## First validation protocol
Compare bio-pattern-inspired and conventional radiator surfaces under identical spectral and thermal boundaries. Measure emissivity/absorptance, thermal transients, degradation and mass per radiated watt.

## Falsification criteria
Reject the advantage if geometry does not improve net rejection or if contamination, cycling, radiator area or mass erases the benefit.

## Limitations and safety boundary
A visually biological surface is not a living thermal system. Performance must be demonstrated radiatively in the spacecraft thermal environment.

## Initial source direction
2025–2026 reviews on bio-inspired radiative thermal management; primary material measurements required for quantitative claims.

**Status:** Proposed module. No experiment or numerical validation is claimed here.
