# Regenerative Biosphere and Life-Support Networks

**Classification:** B/H/A; terrestrial and laboratory components exist, while fully regenerative long-duration space operation remains a development challenge

## Research objective
Determine when a regenerative ecological loop reduces external resupply mass and risk after including reactors, buffers, illumination, gas exchange, purification and control equipment.

## Biological principle
Microbial, algal and higher-plant compartments can transform organic residues, CO2, nitrogen compounds, water and minerals. The engineering object is a compartmentalized ecosystem with measured inputs, outputs and bypasses, not simply plants inside a habitat.

## Engineering scope and system boundary
Model a crew demand vector, organic waste, CO2, water, nutrient ions, oxygen and edible biomass. Separate biological conversion from mechanical pretreatment, filtration, wet oxidation, sterilization and polishing. Include reserve stores and isolation for life-critical flows. Use an abiotic reference that meets identical air, water and food requirements.

## Mathematical formulation

Use the balance dot(x)=S_B v_B+S_A v_A+b-Lx. Close elemental balances over production, consumption, storage and losses. Define recovery rho_i=R_i/D_i only for matched quality and system boundaries. Require every delivered service q_i(t) to remain above its declared minimum. Stability is not equivalent to a high recovery percentage.

## Parameters requiring evidence
Biomass productivity, gas-transfer rate, nutrient uptake, waste conversion yield, water recovery, crew-demand distributions, reactor mass, buffer capacity, lighting/pumping power, contamination probability and maintenance interval. Every parameter needs source, range, environmental condition and scale-transfer caveat.

## First validation protocol
Reconstruct a published regenerative-life-support flow model. Perform elemental-balance closure, step-load tests, reactor-outage tests, delayed nutrient recovery, and recovery after loss of one compartment. Compare total imported mass and power against an abiotic architecture for the same delivered service.

## Falsification criteria
Reject an advantage if the loop cannot meet minimum service under specified disturbances, if external makeup mass or lifecycle power is worse than the reference, or if the conclusion requires assumed near-perfect closure without measured support.

## Limitations and safety boundary
This does not claim a fully closed space biosphere. Crop systems, microbial loops and crew safety require separate validation. Food, air and water require rigorous microbiological and toxicological controls.

## Initial source direction
ESA MELiSSA closed-loop concept and compartment descriptions; ESA 2026 circular-life-support programme; NASA BioNutrients as an on-demand manufacturing comparator.

**Status:** Proposed module. No experiment or numerical validation is claimed here.
