# On-Demand Biological Manufacturing in Space

**Classification:** B/H; NASA flight experiments and terrestrial biomanufacturing provide direct evidence for selected products

## Research objective
Compare biological production of selected nutrients, proteins, polymers or chemical precursors with carried inventory and abiotic production.

## Biological principle
Microbes and enzymes synthesize complex products under relatively mild conditions. Production may use stored substrates or locally sourced carbon, nitrogen and minerals. Product quality, purification and shelf stability must be included.

## Engineering scope and system boundary
Start with nutrients and a few well-characterized chemical products. Separate food-grade output from industrial precursors. Include sterilization, waste handling, downstream purification, shelf life and crew workload. Do not assume all local feedstocks are biologically available.

## Mathematical formulation

For intracellular steady-state flux v and stoichiometric matrix S_met, use S_met v=0 with uptake, reaction and maintenance bounds. Optimize a declared product flux subject to substrate and energy constraints. Couple pathway yield to feedstock preparation, reactor power, product recovery, quality and storage degradation.

## Parameters requiring evidence
Substrate composition, uptake limits, growth rate, product flux, maintenance energy, yield, contamination probability, reactor volume, shelf life, product purity, downstream recovery, crew time and storage temperature.

## First validation protocol
Reconstruct NASA BioNutrients outcomes and selected terrestrial fermentations. Compare production with shipping equivalent usable product and with abiotic synthesis. Use delivered-product functional units, not raw biomass mass.

## Falsification criteria
Reject the advantage if usable yield, purity or shelf life is insufficient, or if total payload plus process system mass/power exceeds the stored-product baseline.

## Limitations and safety boundary
NASA BioNutrients demonstrates selected on-demand microbial products; it does not prove arbitrary pharmaceuticals or complex materials can be manufactured in space.

## Initial source direction
NASA Ames BioNutrients flight experiment page; peer-reviewed space biomanufacturing and metabolic engineering studies to be expanded.
