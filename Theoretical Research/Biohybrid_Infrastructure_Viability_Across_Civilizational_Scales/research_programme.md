# Research Programme Charter

## Working title

**Biohybrid Infrastructure Viability Across Civilizational Scales: A Thermodynamic–Ecological Network Framework from Planetary Life Support to Stellar Swarms**

## Objective

Develop a common, quantitatively auditable framework for choosing between biological (B), biohybrid (H), biomimetic (M) and conventional abiotic (A) infrastructure modules. The goal is not to make everything biological. It is to locate the operating conditions under which biological functions justify their additional mass, process time, sensitivity, containment, maintenance and integration burden.

The 110-concept atlas is a design-space map. The scientific contribution must come from precise architecture definitions, conservation-consistent models, fair baselines, evidence-calibrated uncertainty and falsifiable selection rules.

## Central question

For a specified mission or industrial function, what is the Pareto-optimal allocation of B/H/M/A modules subject to mass, energy, exergy, heat rejection, material, reliability, time, safety and environmental constraints?

## Falsifiable hypotheses

**H1 — Hybrid-nondominance.** At least one realistic, data-supported domain exists in which a hybrid architecture is Pareto-nondominated relative to both B-only and A-only baselines. The domain must be found, not assumed.

**H2 — Boundary-sensitive allocation.** The optimal biological fraction depends on environmental conditions, feedstock, energy availability, process timescale, containment, maintenance and downstream separation. It is not expected to increase monotonically with civilization scale.

**H3 — Locality of biology.** In vacuum-exposed or high-temperature infrastructure, living functions are expected to remain localized in protected process volumes, while biomimetic nonliving components may scale into collectors, radiators and autonomous machines.

**H4 — System-boundary reversal.** Some apparent reactor-level biological advantages may disappear after counting feedstock production, illumination, gas transfer, purification, contamination control, redundancy and replacement logistics.

## Eighteen participating disciplines

1. Synthetic biology — engineered functions and controlled production hosts.
2. Systems biology — coupled biological states and regulation.
3. Photosynthesis — photon capture, charge separation and carbon fixation.
4. Bioelectrochemistry — electron transfer and electrochemical interfaces.
5. Metabolic engineering — pathway flux and product yield.
6. Microbiology — conversion, leaching, digestion and bioremediation.
7. Biomaterials — biological and biohybrid material properties.
8. Biomineralization — mineral precipitation, binding and repair.
9. Mycology — fungal composites and hyphal-network structure.
10. Ecology — stability, diversity and material cycles.
11. Bioprocess engineering — reactor design and scale-up.
12. Chemical engineering — transport, separations and reaction engineering.
13. Materials science — strength, fracture, radiation and thermal properties.
14. Aerospace engineering — orbital mechanics, vacuum and habitat constraints.
15. Robotics — autonomous construction, inspection and repair.
16. Control theory — stability, fault tolerance and constrained feedback.
17. Thermodynamics — energy, exergy, entropy and waste-heat limits.
18. Artificial intelligence — surrogate modelling, design-space exploration and robust control.

A discipline is included in an individual model only when it supplies a real variable, constraint, method or validation requirement.

## Civilizational scale coordinate

Use the Sagan-style continuous coordinate

\[
K=\frac{\log_{10}(P/\mathrm W)-6}{10},
\]

where \(P\) is the power attributed to a civilization in watts. This is a scenario coordinate, not a prediction, welfare metric or universal measure of technology. Its value depends on the energy-accounting convention.

The original Kardashev framework described three broad types. Type IV–VI are not standard scientific categories and must be labelled as nonstandard extrapolations.

## Engineering classes

- **B — Living biological:** the functional conversion, sensing or repair process is performed by viable organisms, cells or enzymes.
- **H — Biohybrid:** biological catalysts or organisms are coupled to engineered materials, semiconductors, electrodes, reactors or control hardware.
- **M — Biomimetic:** a nonliving material, algorithm or machine borrows a biological structural or control principle.
- **A — Conventional abiotic:** physical, chemical, electrochemical or mechanical processes without a biological functional component.

Classification is per module and per version. Hybrid systems can contain multiple classes.

## Core deliverable

A biohybrid infrastructure viability map identifying the conditions (with uncertainty) under which each architecture is feasible, safe and Pareto-nondominated. The first model will focus on a small number of evidence-rich subsystems. The full 110-entry atlas is not itself the model.

## Out of scope

- Claiming practical Dyson-sphere or galactic construction capability.
- Releasing engineered organisms into open planetary environments.
- Treating synthetic data as experimental evidence.
- Treating oxygen production as net energy generation.
- Counting recycled mass without energy and separation costs.
- Presenting a concept as a technology merely because a biological analogy exists.
- Making medical, ecological release or planetary-protection claims without dedicated evidence and safety review.
