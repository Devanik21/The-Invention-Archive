\section*{Abstract}
Cellular rejuvenation is a constrained state-transition problem: age-associated molecular signatures may be shifted toward younger reference states, yet a useful intervention must preserve lineage-specific function, avoid malignant or dysplastic transitions, remain reachable with available perturbations, and not assume that transcriptomic similarity is equivalent to organismal rejuvenation. We propose \emph{Counterdiabatic Sheaf Transport} (CST), a mathematical and computational framework for testing whether geometry-aware, identity-constrained control improves this trade-off over unconstrained state matching. The framework combines four ideas: (i) an information-geometric representation of multimodal cell states; (ii) an asymmetric Randers-Finsler path cost whose directional term is estimated from data rather than equated with entropy production; (iii) a cellular-sheaf consistency residual that operationalizes, but does not claim to prove, preservation of regulatory coherence; and (iv) a port-Hamiltonian-inspired dynamical model coupled to bounded, reachability-aware control. We derive the mathematical conditions under which each component has its intended interpretation and identify several tempting but invalid inferences: sheaf cohomology is not a certificate against cancer, a learned Hamiltonian is not automatically thermodynamic energy, and an inverse control formula is not an exact controller unless the requested velocity lies in the actuation range. A synthetic four-state dynamical example illustrates a generic trade-off: an aggressive intervention reaches a lower abstract age coordinate while causing larger identity-deviation and risk-proxy excursions; a constrained pulsed policy gives up some apparent endpoint improvement while remaining closer to illustrative guardrails. A 300-replicate parameter/noise stress test reproduces this qualitative trade-off in the chosen toy model. These results are computational demonstrations of the code and assumptions, not evidence about real cells. We specify a falsifiable empirical programme using existing single-cell aging atlases, time-resolved reprogramming datasets, chromatin-contact assays, and functional endpoints. The framework should be rejected or simplified if directional geometry, regulatory-consistency penalties, or structured control fail to improve held-out predictions or experimentally measured identity/function at matched rejuvenation. Its proposed contribution is thus an auditable integration and validation design, not a claim that rejuvenation, Finsler geometry, sheaf theory, or counterdiabatic control is individually new.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} partial cellular reprogramming; epigenetic aging; information geometry; Randers-Finsler metric; cellular sheaves; Hodge Laplacian; port-Hamiltonian neural ODE; constrained control; lineage identity; reproducible computational biology.

\section{Introduction: rejuvenation as a constrained transition}
Aging is not a single molecular variable. It involves interacting processes including genomic instability, epigenetic alterations, loss of proteostasis, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, altered intercellular communication, stem-cell exhaustion, and other changes summarized in contemporary accounts of the hallmarks of aging \cite{LopezOtin2023}. These processes differ across cell types, tissues, and individuals. A method that optimizes one molecular signature can fail to improve tissue function, and a method that increases plasticity can compromise the stable identity on which tissue architecture depends.

Partial reprogramming has established that some aging-associated molecular features can be altered by transient expression of reprogramming factors in selected experimental settings. Ocampo and colleagues reported amelioration of age-associated hallmarks in a premature-aging mouse model and related effects in older wild-type mice \cite{Ocampo2016}. Lu and colleagues reported restoration of youthful methylation and transcriptomic features together with improved regeneration and vision-related outcomes in mouse models using OSK-based reprogramming \cite{Lu2020}. Browder and colleagues found that the duration of in-vivo partial-reprogramming regimens influenced outcomes in physiologically aged mice \cite{Browder2022}. Such studies justify asking how trajectories can be made controllable and measurable. They do not show that there is one universally safe rejuvenation trajectory, nor that all age-associated information can or should be reset.

The present proposal reframes the computational problem. Let a cell's latent molecular state be $x(t)$ and let $\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{young}}$ denote a prespecified distribution of reference states for the relevant lineage, assay, sex, age window, and tissue context. A naive objective minimizes a distance to this target. A more responsible objective must also penalize loss of lineage markers, regulatory inconsistency, excessive control effort, dysplastic or oncogenic signatures, functional decline, and uncertainty. In practice, these requirements are partially observed and cannot all be compressed into one epigenetic-clock score.

We propose \emph{Counterdiabatic Sheaf Transport} (CST) as an integrative model architecture, not a biological law. In CST, a path is evaluated with a direction-sensitive geometry; regulatory agreement is represented through a cellular sheaf; dynamics are modelled with an energy-accounting state-space architecture; and an auxiliary controller attempts to track a target path subject to actuation and safety constraints. Each component is tested against simpler alternatives so that complexity can be removed when it fails to help.

\subsection{Research questions}
The paper is organized around five questions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can direction-dependent path geometry improve the prediction of observed cell-state transitions beyond Euclidean or Riemannian baselines?
\item Can a sheaf-based regulatory-consistency penalty add information about lineage preservation beyond marker-gene and functional assays?
\item Can a port-Hamiltonian-inspired model improve model calibration or control robustness relative to an unconstrained neural ODE or a conventional state-space model?
\item Under what controllability conditions can a counterdiabatic-style auxiliary input track a desired trajectory, and when must the framework explicitly declare the target unreachable?
\item Do these components improve held-out rejuvenation-versus-identity trade-offs in biological data, rather than only in a synthetic model constructed to exhibit them?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions and claims ledger}
The contributions are methodological and conditional.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A typed latent-state model.} We distinguish observed assays, latent molecular state, lineage identity, age-associated endpoints, and safety endpoints rather than identifying them with one another.
\item \textbf{A defensible directional cost.} We specify a Randers-Finsler metric with a data-estimated directional covector and the regularity condition required for a valid Randers norm.
\item \textbf{An operational use of sheaf consistency.} We use the coboundary residual and harmonics as structural descriptors, with independent lineage markers and functional assays as non-negotiable validators.
\item \textbf{A reachability-aware control formulation.} We replace an unconditional pseudoinverse formula with a constrained least-squares controller and explicit feasibility tests.
\item \textbf{A reproducible synthetic falsification demonstration.} Public code tests the stated toy model and quantifies the trade-off created by chosen assumptions; no biological conclusions are inferred from it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Prior art and the boundary of novelty}
A serious framework must acknowledge the mature traditions it borrows from. This paper is built by connecting these traditions in a testable way; it does not claim to have invented them.

\subsection{Epigenetic aging and partial reprogramming}
DNA methylation clocks and cell-type-specific aging signatures have made it possible to quantify some age-associated molecular changes, but such measures are not complete descriptions of biological age or function. The Tabula Muris Senis consortium generated a single-cell transcriptomic atlas across the lifespan of mice covering 23 tissues, providing a foundational resource for cross-tissue aging analyses \cite{TabulaMuris2020}. The atlas also illustrates a key modeling constraint: cellular composition and within-cell expression can both change with age. Analyses that do not separate these effects can confuse shifts in cell population proportions with within-lineage state transitions.

Partial cellular reprogramming is likewise prior art. Ocampo et al., Lu et al., and Browder et al. report encouraging but context-bounded results in mice \cite{Ocampo2016,Lu2020,Browder2022}. Reviews emphasize both the potential of partial reprogramming and remaining questions about mechanism, durability, and safety \cite{Avelar2025,LongWinding2024}. CST does not frame OSKM as a uniformly harmful sledgehammer: outcomes depend on factors, delivery, duration, tissue, and biological context. The framework instead asks how to quantify a target state change while separately observing lineage and safety.

\subsection{Chromatin organization and phase separation}
The idea that chromatin organization is influenced by condensate-like phase behavior is supported by experiments on HP1 and heterochromatin domain formation \cite{Larson2017,Strom2017}. Reviews discuss roles of phase separation and the inheritance of repressive chromatin states \cite{Akilli2024,LLPSAging2024}. However, the broad analogy between age-related chromatin change and spinodal coarsening is not sufficient to prove that a particular thermodynamic phase-separation process drives aging in vivo. CST therefore treats chromatin condensation, domain boundaries, and associated features as potential observed coordinates, not as a universal mechanism. A claimed phase transition would require measured order parameters, finite-size and noise analyses, hysteresis or scaling signatures, and independent replication.

\subsection{Mitochondria, metabolism, and nuclear state}
Mitochondrial metabolites and stress signals participate in mitochondria-to-nucleus communication and can influence epigenetic regulation \cite{Zhu2022}. Circadian NAD$^+$ metabolism has also been linked to chromatin remodeling \cite{NADChromatin2016}. These data motivate measurement of metabolic context and temporal variation. They do not establish the stronger chain proposed in the original concept: mitochondrial membrane-potential and NAD$^+$/NADH rhythms phase-lock all chromatin organization, their decoherence causes spinodal coarsening, and this is the primary arrow of biological aging. CST treats that causal chain as a specific hypothesis to test against alternatives, not a premise to assume.

\subsection{Information geometry and optimal transport}
Information geometry supplies metrics on statistical models, including Fisher information metrics. In cell biology, single-cell state transitions have been analyzed using optimal transport and inferred temporal trajectories \cite{Schiebinger2019}, and RNA velocity estimates short-term transcriptional dynamics from spliced and unspliced RNA under model assumptions \cite{LaManno2018,scVelo2020}. Importantly, a recent 2026 study introduced SCIM, using Fisher information geometry and RNA velocity to quantify cell-phenotype transition manifolds \cite{Huang2026}. Thus, using information geometry for cell-state trajectories is not new. CST's question is whether an explicitly asymmetric path cost, tied to a separately measured drift field and combined with structural and safety constraints, adds predictive or control value beyond these precedents.

\subsection{Cellular sheaves and cohomology}
Cellular sheaves have been developed as a way to represent local data and compatibility conditions across a cell complex. Hansen and Ghrist provide a spectral treatment relating sheaf Laplacians to cohomology \cite{HansenGhrist2019}. In this paper, a sheaf assigns vector spaces to regulatory loci and complexes and restriction maps that encode a selected abstraction of consistency across regulatory relations. This is a new proposed application within CST, but no priority claim is made that it is the first possible use of a sheaf in gene regulation, and no claim is made that cohomology constitutes a sufficient representation of biological identity.

\subsection{Counterdiabatic control and port-Hamiltonian learning}
Counterdiabatic driving originated in quantum control as a method for tracking prescribed state changes while suppressing unwanted transitions, and approximate protocols can be derived under constraints on available control terms \cite{Sels2017}. Exact tracking is not generally available under arbitrary actuation constraints. Port-Hamiltonian systems separate interconnection structure, dissipation, and input ports; learned port-Hamiltonian neural networks have been used to model time-dependent dynamical systems \cite{Desai2021,Cherifi2020}. CST transfers these modeling principles into a proposed cell-state control problem. Such an analogy is mathematical, not evidence that a cell literally obeys a quantum adiabatic theorem or that the learned Hamiltonian is physical free energy.

\subsection{Novelty statement}
The defensible novelty claim is a \emph{specific, falsifiable integration proposal}: an asymmetric state-space path cost, sheaf-based regulatory-consistency diagnostics, port-Hamiltonian-inspired dynamics, reachability-aware counterdiabatic-style control, and independent identity/function/safety tests are evaluated as one framework. A scoped literature search through 14 October 2026 found close prior work on every component and direct precedent for information-geometric cell manifolds. We did not perform a formal systematic review of all publications, patent filings, or unpublished work. Absolute novelty and priority are therefore unverified. The core result of this paper is the explicit formulation and validation plan rather than a claim of experimental discovery.

\section{Problem formulation and assumptions}
Let $c$ index a cell type or lineage, $d$ a donor or biological replicate, and $t$ a measured time or age. The desired state is not an arbitrary low-age point in latent space; it is a reference distribution $\mu_{\mathrm{young},c}$ conditional on lineage and assay. The state at time $t$ is latent and only partially observed. Define
\begin{equation}
Y_t = \mathcal{H}(x_t, z_t, q_t; \eta) + \epsilon_t,
\label{eq:observation}
\end{equation}
where $x_t$ is the molecular state, $z_t$ denotes external and metabolic context, $q_t$ denotes functional phenotype, $\eta$ denotes platform and batch effects, and $\epsilon_t$ denotes measurement noise. $Y_t$ may include RNA counts, chromatin accessibility, chromatin contacts, histone/DNA modifications, metabolite measurements, imaging features, and functional readouts. These modalities have different noise models and cannot be treated as interchangeable coordinates without a validated integration method.

We use a low-dimensional latent representation $x_t\in\M\subseteq\R^d$ only as an approximation to the relevant state manifold. The latent chart may be local and can fail across poorly sampled transitions. A coordinate transformation should not change a properly defined geometric result; neural embeddings and dimension reduction, in contrast, can distort distances and orientation unless checked. The target distribution, features, normalization, and state-estimation procedure must be frozen before held-out evaluation.

\subsection{Assumption ledger}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Measurement sufficiency (A1).} The measured features resolve the molecular and functional dimensions material to the tested lineage and intervention. This assumption is unlikely to hold universally; it must be tested with residual diagnostics and additional modalities.
\item \textbf{Local smoothness (A2).} Within a restricted state region, the latent manifold admits a local smooth metric and differentiable drift approximation. Discrete fate switches, clonal selection, and rare states may violate it.
\item \textbf{Directional identifiability (A3).} Directional bias is estimated from time-ordered or perturbation data, not inferred from cross-sectional age labels alone.
\item \textbf{Regulatory graph reliability (A4).} The regulatory relations used to define sheaf maps are sufficiently supported by perturbation, chromatin-contact, or carefully qualified evidence. Correlation alone is not causal dependence.
\item \textbf{Control observability and reachability (A5).} The state variables needed for constraints are observable at the relevant update cadence, and the intended target is reachable under the available perturbations. Failure of reachability must be reported, not hidden by a large control signal.
\item \textbf{Safety surrogate validity (A6).} The identity and risk surrogates correlate with independent functional and pathological endpoints. They cannot replace direct safety measurement.
\end{enumerate}

The framework is not valid merely because an optimizer converges. The assumptions form a checklist against which the data and the model must be audited. When an assumption fails, the appropriate result is model restriction, uncertainty inflation, or rejection of the relevant component.

\section{Observables, state representation, and identifiability}
The state vector should be constructed around measurable features rather than metaphors. A candidate feature stack includes: (i) cell-type-specific expression modules and transcription-factor activity estimates; (ii) chromatin accessibility and methylation summaries; (iii) 3D contact features such as compartment scores or reproducible domain-level statistics; (iv) chromatin-protein localization or condensate descriptors where valid imaging exists; (v) metabolic context including NAD$^+$/NADH-related measures and mitochondrial function; (vi) independent lineage-specific functional assays; and (vii) adverse-state indicators such as sustained cell-cycle re-entry, DNA-damage responses, loss of lineage markers, apoptosis, and transformation-associated phenotypes. Which measurements are required depends on the biological system.

Not every experiment can measure every modality longitudinally in the same cell. Multi-omic assays often consume the cell, while repeated imaging can perturb the system and may only observe proxies. Consequently, a practical architecture may combine matched populations, lineage tracing, repeated live-cell measurements, and time-staggered destructive assays. The mapping between measurements and latent state must include uncertainty and technical batch effects.

\subsection{Separating chronological age, molecular age, and function}
Three targets must remain distinct:
\begin{itemize}
\item chronological age is an experimental covariate or elapsed time;
\item molecular age is a score trained to correlate with age or a reference molecular state;
\item functional rejuvenation is improvement or restoration of a relevant phenotype, such as contractility, barrier function, electrophysiological fidelity, stress tolerance, or tissue repair.
\end{itemize}
A lower predicted epigenetic age can occur without functional improvement; an age score can also change due to cell-composition or assay-distribution shifts. The primary endpoint in any real experiment must therefore be a prespecified functionally meaningful outcome or a hierarchy of molecular and functional outcomes with controlled multiplicity. CST does not identify a latent age coordinate with literal organismal youth.

\subsection{Direction is not causation}
RNA velocity and temporal optimal transport can supply directional hypotheses, but they depend on sampling, kinetic assumptions, and the observed cell-state distribution. Cross-sectional datasets do not directly identify within-cell trajectories. A vector field fitted from observational single-cell measurements can be confounded by selection, cell death, proliferation, batch differences, and missing states. Perturbation data and repeated time points are required to attribute a transition to a candidate mechanism. In CST, directional geometry will be learned only where there is sufficient evidence for an oriented transition model; otherwise, the Riemannian baseline should be used.

\subsection{Identifiability questions}
The following parameters may be confounded: metric curvature and latent-coordinate distortion; directional drift and uneven sampling; sheaf maps and graph-edge uncertainty; dissipation and unobserved driving; and intervention effectiveness and measurement delay. Identifiability will be evaluated by simulation-based parameter recovery, posterior or profile-likelihood diagnostics, intervention contrasts, and held-out prediction. If multiple parameter sets yield indistinguishable observables but divergent control recommendations, the controller is not identified well enough for intervention ranking.

\section{Directional state geometry: a cautious Randers-Finsler construction}
A Riemannian metric assigns a local quadratic cost to infinitesimal state displacements. A Finsler metric permits a more general direction-dependent norm. A Randers form is
\begin{equation}
F(x,v)=\sqrt{g_{ij}(x)v^iv^j}+\beta_i(x)v^i,
\label{eq:randers}
\end{equation}
where $g(x)$ is positive definite and $\beta(x)$ is a covector field. The standard condition
\begin{equation}
\|\beta(x)\|_{g}:=\sqrt{g^{ij}(x)\beta_i(x)\beta_j(x)}<1
\label{eq:randers_cond}
\end{equation}
ensures that $F$ is positive for nonzero velocities and gives a strongly convex Randers structure under the usual regularity assumptions \cite{Bao2000,Shen2001}. Units must be handled consistently: the norm of the covector must be dimensionless relative to the metric scaling. In practice, the state variables and time need explicit normalization, or scale parameters must be included.

\begin{proposition}[Directional asymmetry of a Randers cost]
For any $x$ and velocity $v$ for which \cref{eq:randers} is defined,
\begin{equation}
F(x,v)-F(x,-v)=2\beta_i(x)v^i.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The quadratic term is even in velocity because $g_{ij}v^iv^j=g_{ij}(-v^i)(-v^j)$. The linear term changes sign. Subtracting gives twice the linear term.\end{proof}

This proposition establishes an algebraic asymmetry; it does not establish that aging is a geodesic or that a fitted $\beta$ is an entropy-production field. The biological hypothesis is narrower: a directional covector estimated from time-resolved transitions may capture a reproducible difference in transition cost between forward and reverse state changes. It should be compared with a symmetric metric and with a directional diffusion/drift model.

\subsection{What is the directional covector?}
We define $\beta$ as an empirically estimated covector field linked to the asymmetry in observed transitions after correcting for sampling and covariates. Estimation might proceed by fitting a local transition model and comparing forward and reverse conditional transition likelihoods, or by minimizing a held-out transition prediction objective under the norm constraint \cref{eq:randers_cond}. The covector is not set equal to entropy production. Entropy production is typically a scalar rate or trajectory functional requiring physical fluxes and thermodynamic forces; it cannot be identified from transcriptomic expression alone. A physically motivated entropy estimate may contribute to a separate cost term if its units, boundaries, and assumptions are documented.

\subsection{Trajectory action}
For a path $\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow\M$ define the geometric action
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{F}[\gamma]=\int_0^T F(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))^2\,dt.
\label{eq:path_action}
\end{equation}
The endpoint target is a distribution, not necessarily a single point. A complete objective can combine geometric path action, target-distribution discrepancy, control effort, and penalties:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{J}[\gamma,u] ={}& d_{F,T}(\gamma(T),\mu_{\mathrm{young},c})^2 + \lambda_F\mathcal{A}_{F}[\gamma]
+\lambda_u\int_0^T \|u(t)\|^2\,dt \nonumber\\
&+\lambda_I\int_0^T \pos{r_I(\gamma(t))-\epsilon_I}^2\,dt
+\lambda_R\int_0^T \pos{r_R(\gamma(t))-\epsilon_R}^2\,dt.
\label{eq:joint_obj}
\end{align}
Here $d_{F,T}$ is a target discrepancy under the chosen geometry, $r_I$ is the identity-surrogate residual and $r_R$ is the risk-surrogate residual. The nonnegative weights are tuning parameters selected without leaking held-out outcomes. $\mathcal{A}_F$ is a path-regularity cost, not automatically physical work. Actual intervention work, ATP consumption, entropy production, or toxicity must be measured separately when available.

\subsection{Interpretation of the asymmetry}
If the estimated $\beta$ is stable, improves held-out transition prediction, and remains directionally consistent across technical and biological replicates, it may help characterize barriers to state reversal. If the estimated directional term is unstable across resampling, changes sign under plausible embeddings, or gives no incremental predictive value, its contribution should be removed. A nonzero $\beta$ can also arise from incomplete sampling or nonstationary conditions; directionality alone is not evidence of irreversibility at the microscopic level.

\section{A regulatory cellular sheaf as an identity diagnostic}
A cellular sheaf assigns vector spaces to cells of a graph or cell complex and linear restriction maps that express how local data are compared on shared structure. In CST, let $\G$ be a finite complex derived from a regulatory graph or hypergraph. For each vertex $v$, let $\Fsh(v)$ be a vector space of local regulatory features; for each edge or higher-dimensional cell $e$, let $\Fsh(e)$ be a space of compatibility summaries. A restriction map $\rho_{v\to e}:\Fsh(v)\rightarrow\Fsh(e)$ encodes the agreed representation of a local feature on that regulatory relation.

For simplicity, consider a graph-level cellular sheaf. The degree-zero cochain space is
\begin{equation}
C^0(\G;\Fsh)=\bigoplus_{v\in V}\Fsh(v),\qquad
C^1(\G;\Fsh)=\bigoplus_{e\in E}\Fsh(e).
\end{equation}
The coboundary operator $\delta^0:C^0\rightarrow C^1$ computes disagreement between restrictions of local sections. The degree-zero sheaf Laplacian is
\begin{equation}
L_{\Fsh}^{0}=(\delta^0)^*\delta^0,
\label{eq:sheaf_laplacian}
\end{equation}
where $*$ is the adjoint under the selected inner products. Then
\begin{equation}
\ker(L_{\Fsh}^{0})=\ker(\delta^0)\cong H^0(\G;\Fsh),
\label{eq:H0}
\end{equation}
for this finite-dimensional setting. Elements of this kernel are globally consistent sections under the specified local restrictions \cite{HansenGhrist2019}.

\subsection{What sheaf consistency does and does not mean}
The equation above is a mathematical description of consistency given a chosen graph, feature spaces, maps, and inner products. Biological interpretation is supplied by how those ingredients are constructed and validated. If edges are based on correlations, the sheaf residual measures agreement under correlational constraints, not causal regulatory control. If the graph excludes important enhancers, transcription factors, or feedback loops, a low residual may only indicate that the model omitted disagreements. If the sheaf is rebuilt after treatment, a changing kernel can make apparent invariance vacuous unless the comparison is transported through a documented correspondence.

Most importantly, \textbf{$H^0$ preservation does not prove that a cell cannot become cancerous}. Neoplastic transformation can occur through mutations, genomic instability, altered signaling, epigenetic changes, or other routes not represented in the sheaf. It is invalid to describe a sheaf cohomology invariant as a hard theorem of oncological safety. CST uses the sheaf as one regulatory-consistency diagnostic, to be triangulated with independent lineage markers, cell-cycle behavior, genome stability, morphology, and functional assays.

\subsection{Identity reference and admissible set}
Let $z=\Phi(x)$ map the latent state into the cochain representation. A reference sheaf and reference section set $\mathcal{Q}_{c}$ are constructed for lineage $c$ from multiple healthy reference donors and, where appropriate, known functional states. Define
\begin{equation}
r_I(x)=\underbrace{\|\delta^0\Phi(x)\|_{W}^{2}}_{\text{regulatory inconsistency}}
+\lambda_m\underbrace{d_m(\Phi(x),\mathcal{Q}_{c})^2}_{\text{distance to reference identity}}
+\lambda_f\underbrace{d_f(q(x),\mathcal{Q}_{c}^{\mathrm{func}})^2}_{\text{functional deviation}},
\label{eq:identity_residual}
\end{equation}
where $W$ is a declared positive-semidefinite weighting matrix and the last two terms compare molecular markers and independent function with a lineage-specific reference. The admissible identity region is
\begin{equation}
\Iset=\{x:r_I(x)\leq\epsilon_I\}.
\end{equation}
This is more defensible than requiring a raw coordinate vector to lie in the orthogonal complement of a fixed harmonic subspace. It also acknowledges that healthy states vary and that perfect preservation of every baseline coordinate would prevent legitimate adaptation.

\subsection{A limitation of hard projection}
Projection onto $\ker L_{\Fsh}^0$ can be useful as a regularizer, but a hard projection can suppress biologically meaningful responses or amplify errors from a misspecified graph. It can also be discontinuous if the estimated rank of $L$ changes with noise or treatment. We therefore recommend a soft residual penalty and a sensitivity analysis over graph construction, edge confidence, and regularization. A hard invariant should only be introduced when external evidence supports it and the chosen state representation demonstrates stable, meaningful equivalence across conditions.

\section{Port-Hamiltonian-inspired cell dynamics}
Port-Hamiltonian systems decompose dynamics into interconnection, dissipation, and external input. For a differentiable scalar function $H_\theta(x)$, define
\begin{equation}
\dot{x}=\big(J_\theta(x)-R_\theta(x)\big)\nabla H_\theta(x)+B_\theta(x)u+d_\theta(x,z)+\xi(t),
\label{eq:ph}
\end{equation}
where $J_\theta(x)=-J_\theta(x)^\top$, $R_\theta(x)\succeq0$, $B_\theta$ is an input matrix, $d_\theta$ represents measured context or unmodeled drift, and $\xi$ is process noise. With output $y=B_\theta^\top\nabla H_\theta$, in a deterministic time-invariant model with $d=0$ and no noise,
\begin{equation}
\dot H_\theta=-\nabla H_\theta^\top R_\theta\nabla H_\theta+y^\top u.
\label{eq:energy_balance}
\end{equation}
The skew-symmetric interconnection contributes no net change to $H_\theta$ because $v^\top Jv=0$ for any real vector $v$; the $R$ term is nonpositive; the input port may add or remove energy relative to this learned function.

The model is useful because it constrains the learned dynamics instead of allowing an arbitrary neural vector field. But a learned $H_\theta$ is not automatically measured Gibbs free energy, ATP cost, or cellular enthalpy. Unless the model is grounded in physically calibrated state variables and energetic measurements, $H_\theta$ should be described as a learned storage/Lyapunov-like function. Likewise, $J$ and $R$ are a parameterization of an inductive bias; fitting them does not establish that mitochondria are a conservative oscillator and chromatin drift is a purely dissipative process.

\subsection{Metabolic and phase variables as candidate features}
A candidate state may include oscillator phases $\vartheta_k$ for metabolic or transcriptional signals. A Kuramoto-inspired summary is
\begin{equation}
Z(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}w_k\ee^{\ii\vartheta_k(t)},\qquad r_{\phi}(t)=1-|Z(t)|,
\label{eq:phase_order}
\end{equation}
where weights $w_k$ and phase definitions are specified before testing. This is only meaningful when time series support stable phase estimation; single snapshots cannot recover oscillation phase. The synchrony statistic is not a universal aging measure. It should be evaluated as an additional covariate and tested for incremental predictive value over metabolite levels, circadian phase, mitochondrial function, cell cycle, and environmental context.

\subsection{Model classes and baselines}
We recommend three nested model classes: (1) a conventional state-space or neural ODE without structured energy constraints; (2) the port-Hamiltonian-inspired model with input-output structure; and (3) the same model plus candidate phase features. All should share comparable data splits, parameter budgets where possible, uncertainty calibration, and missing-modality handling. A structured model should be retained only if it improves out-of-sample prediction, calibration, or intervention-effect estimation, not because it looks more physically sophisticated.

\section{Counterdiabatic-style control with reachability checks}
Suppose the learned uncontrolled drift is
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(x,z)=\big(J_\theta(x)-R_\theta(x)\big)\nabla H_\theta(x)+d_\theta(x,z).
\end{equation}
Given a proposed reference path $\gamma_*(t)$, the instantaneous residual velocity is $r(t)=\dot\gamma_*(t)-f_\theta(\gamma_*(t),z_t)$. A formal exact input would solve $B_\theta(x)u=r$. This is feasible only if $r\in\operatorname{Range}(B_\theta(x))$. A Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse returns a least-squares solution when exact matching is impossible; it does not guarantee safety, boundedness, or feasibility.

We therefore define an approximate control step
\begin{equation}
u^*(t)=\argmin_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{\|B_\theta u-r\|_{W_u}^{2}+\lambda_u\|u\|_2^2\right\}
\label{eq:control_lsq}
\end{equation}
subject to the state dynamics, input amplitude and slew constraints, identity set $x_t\in\Iset$, risk guards, and an uncertainty margin. The allowed input set $\mathcal{U}$ represents real candidate perturbations only after their directions, delivery mechanism, dose range, and observed effects are characterized. It should not mix transcript-factor pulses, bioelectric stimulation, and small molecules as though they were interchangeable controls.

\subsection{Safety constraints are independent of topology}
Let $h_k(x)\leq0$ denote empirically defined guard functions for such outcomes as sustained proliferation, a lineage-specific function score, DNA-damage responses, or transformation-related phenotypes. The set
\begin{equation}
\Ssafe=\{x:r_I(x)\leq\epsilon_I,\ h_k(x)\leq0\text{ for all }k\}
\end{equation}
can be used in model-predictive control or chance-constrained optimization. In a stochastic model, a chance constraint could be written
\begin{equation}
\Pr\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}h_k(x_t)>0\right)\leq\alpha_k.
\end{equation}
A guarantee is only as credible as the probability model, calibration, coverage of rare events, and validation data. Without a sufficient biological safety dataset, these are design targets rather than certified bounds. No in-silico model can establish absence of tumorigenicity.

\subsection{The control target may be infeasible}
Identity preservation, target closeness, and low control energy can be mutually incompatible. The framework must report the attainable Pareto frontier rather than return a single overconfident ``optimal'' intervention. A solution should be declared infeasible if no allowed control reaches a prespecified target while satisfying constraints under uncertainty. The target can then be relaxed only through a transparent scientific decision and a preregistered revision, not by silent tuning after seeing outcomes.

\section{Joint optimization problem}
Let $u(\cdot)$ be an admissible control and let $x(\cdot)$ satisfy the model. A risk-aware objective is
\begin{align}
\min_{u(\cdot)}\quad & \E\left[ D_{\mathrm{target}}(x_T,\mu_{\mathrm{young},c}) + \lambda_F\int_0^T F(x_t,\dot{x}_t)^2\,dt \right.\nonumber\\
&\left.+\lambda_u\int_0^T\|u_t\|^2dt+\lambda_I\int_0^T\pos{r_I(x_t)-\epsilon_I}^2dt\right.\nonumber\\
&\left.+\lambda_R\int_0^T\pos{r_R(x_t)-\epsilon_R}^2dt+\lambda_S\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{uncertainty}}(u)\right]
\label{eq:objective_full}
\end{align}
subject to \cref{eq:ph}, $u_t\in\mathcal{U}$, initial state distribution $x_0\sim\mu_{\mathrm{aged},c}$, identity constraints, and risk guardrails. $D_{\mathrm{target}}$ must not be chosen solely because it is easy for the model to optimize; at least one endpoint must remain independent of the state representation used for control.

A robust version optimizes against a set of plausible dynamics $\Theta$ or worst-case risk:
\begin{equation}
\min_u\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathcal{J}_{\theta}(u),\qquad x_t\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{safe},\theta}\text{ for plausible }\theta.
\end{equation}
This version may be computationally expensive and can produce conservative controls. A practical study should begin with small systems and compare nominal model predictive control, constrained linear-quadratic control where applicable, a neural ODE policy, and a simple pulsed baseline. The counterdiabatic component has value only if it improves the risk-benefit frontier under matched targets and comparable control resources.

\subsection{Proposed algorithm}
The algorithm is modular by design. The state estimator produces a posterior distribution rather than one exact $x_t$. The geometry module estimates the metric and directional covector. The sheaf module produces a consistency residual and its uncertainty. The dynamics module predicts states under candidate inputs. The optimizer selects a bounded action or declares infeasibility. During the control proposal, no action is applied to a biological system until independent experimentation has calibrated the relevant transitions and guard functions.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{CST inference and candidate-control loop (research blueprint)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Matched time-resolved data $Y$, lineage label $c$, reference distributions, candidate input set $\mathcal{U}$
\Ensure Predictive model, risk/identity diagnostics, and either a candidate policy or an infeasibility report
\State Fit a measurement model and latent state estimator with donor/study-aware validation.
\State Estimate the information metric $g$ and test a symmetric baseline before fitting directional $\beta$.
\State Retain $\beta$ only if regularity, directionality, and held-out prediction checks pass.
\State Construct a documented regulatory complex $\G$ and uncertain restriction maps; compute $r_I(x)$.
\State Fit unconstrained and port-Hamiltonian-inspired dynamics with identical data splits.
\State Calibrate risk surrogates using independent functional and pathological observations where available.
\State Propose a reference path from the aged-state distribution toward the lineage-matched target distribution.
\State Solve the bounded least-squares control problem with identity and risk guards.
\If{reachability or uncertainty constraints fail}
  \State Return ``infeasible / insufficiently identified'' and the active failure reason.
\Else
  \State Return candidate schedule, uncertainty envelope, model version, and preregistered validation tests.
\EndIf
\State Evaluate only on held-out donors, perturbations, and endpoints; record all negative results and ablations.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Synthetic demonstration: methods and results}
\textbf{Data status.} This section uses a hand-constructed four-state differential equation and a fixed random seed. No biological data, measured parameter, training atlas, or published single-cell count was used. The numerical experiment exists to verify code execution and to expose the consequences of selected structural assumptions. It is not evidence that CST predicts or safely controls living cells.

\subsection{Toy dynamics}
The dimensionless state is $x=(a,\ell,r,\phi)$, where $a$ is an abstract age-associated coordinate (lower is labelled the target direction), $\ell$ is an identity-deviation proxy, $r$ is an oncogenic-stress proxy, and $\phi$ is a phase-decoherence proxy. These names are labels within the toy system, not biological biomarkers. The dynamics are
\begin{align}
\dot a &= k_a+k_{a\phi}\phi-\gamma_a a-\eta_a u,\\
\dot\ell&=-\gamma_\ell\ell+\eta_\ell u^2-\rho_\ell v,\\
\dot r&=-\gamma_r r+\eta_r u^2-\rho_r v,\\
\dot\phi&=-\gamma_\phi\phi+\eta_\phi u,
\label{eq:toy}
\end{align}
where $u\geq0$ is a generic reprogramming input and $v\geq0$ is a generic repair input. The quadratic coupling $u^2$ means high inputs carry larger identity- and risk-proxy costs in this deliberately chosen example. Nothing in biology compels this exact equation; different models may produce different rankings.

The starting state is $(1.0,0.02,0.01,0.18)$ and the integration interval is $T=12$ dimensionless time units with step $\Delta t=0.01$. The base coefficients are $k_a=0.018$, $k_{a\phi}=0.040$, $\gamma_a=0.10$, $\eta_a=0.24$, $\gamma_\ell=0.40$, $\eta_\ell=0.55$, $\rho_\ell=0.45$, $\gamma_r=0.38$, $\eta_r=0.45$, $\rho_r=0.35$, $\gamma_\phi=0.20$, and $\eta_\phi=0.25$. All coefficients are synthetic and unitless. The baseline has no control; the aggressive protocol applies $u=0.90$ from $t=0.4$ through $4.4$ without repair; the constrained protocol applies $u=0.62$ in pulses when the identity and risk proxies are below illustrative thresholds and uses a feedback repair input. Details are fully specified in \texttt{simulate\_model.py}.
