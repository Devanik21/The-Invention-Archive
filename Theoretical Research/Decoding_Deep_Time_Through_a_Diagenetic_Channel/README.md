# Decoding Deep Time Through a Diagenetic Channel

**An Information-Theoretic Recoverability Framework for Speleothem Climate Archives**  
**Author:** Devanik Debnath · National Institute of Technology Agartala, India  
**Research-process coordination:** Xylia  
**Release date:** 2026-10-18 · **Status:** theoretical and synthetic computational preprint; not peer reviewed

> **Evidence boundary:** This release does not analyze SISAL rows, instrumental cave data, or laboratory measurements. All numerical experiments use synthetic data generated by `verify_model.py`. The results demonstrate properties of the stated simulator only and are not paleoclimate estimates.

## Research question

Can multiple speleothem proxies, paired with fabric/texture metadata, make latent climate more recoverable when diagenetic and microbial contributions are structured and partly observed? Equally important: when is the climate signal not identifiable, regardless of decoder size?

The framework combines existing strands—global proxy compilations, fabric logging, forward proxy-system models, multi-proxy Bayesian reconstruction, and diagenesis studies—into a recoverability-first model and a reproducible synthetic stress test. The novelty claim is deliberately limited to this explicit synthesis and evaluation rule; it is not a claim of absolute priority or an empirically validated new paleoclimate method.

## Main mathematical objects

For climate state \(x_t\), proxy vector \(\mathbf y_t\), diagenetic state \(z_{d,t}\), microbial/provenance state \(z_{m,t}\), and measurement noise \(\boldsymbol\epsilon_t\), a local linear observation model is:

$$
\mathbf y_t = A x_t + B_d z_{d,t} + B_m z_{m,t} + \boldsymbol\epsilon_t.
$$

Observed fabric or texture \(f_t\) is side information about the channel state, with a label likelihood that must be calibrated rather than assumed perfect:

$$
p(f_t\mid z_{d,t},z_{m,t},h_t).
$$

Under the simplified Gaussian model, where climate prior covariance is \(\Sigma_x\), proxy response matrix is \(A\), and residual covariance conditional on fabric is \(\Sigma_n(f)\), posterior covariance is:

$$
\Sigma_{x\mid y,f}=\left(\Sigma_x^{-1}+A^\top\Sigma_n(f)^{-1}A\right)^{-1}.
$$

The conditional mutual information in this Gaussian model is:

$$
I(x;\mathbf y\mid f)=\frac12\log\det\!\left(I+\Sigma_x A^\top\Sigma_n(f)^{-1}A\right).
$$

These are conditional formulas, not values estimated from real caves. If the climate response lies within the nuisance-response subspace, different climate/alteration combinations may yield the same observation. Priors may regularize the posterior, but do not create observational evidence.

## Synthetic benchmark results

The fixed-seed experiment trains on 72 synthetic caves × 170 sequence steps and evaluates on two held-out synthetic scenarios with 3,400 records each. Three OLS references are compared: first proxy only, three proxies, and three proxies plus noisy synthetic fabric labels.

| Scenario | Method | RMSE | 90% interval coverage | Gaussian NLL |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| In-distribution | Single proxy | 0.4289 | 0.8853 | 0.5755 |
| In-distribution | Multi-proxy | 0.2739 | 0.8956 | 0.1242 |
| In-distribution | Channel-aware | 0.2686 | 0.8962 | 0.1047 |
| Shifted alteration | Single proxy | 0.6616 | 0.6721 | 1.3442 |
| Shifted alteration | Multi-proxy | 0.3132 | 0.8356 | 0.2813 |
| Shifted alteration | Channel-aware | 0.3007 | 0.8471 | 0.2351 |

The small extra RMSE improvement from fabric labels is encouraging only within the deliberately assumed simulator. Shifted-scenario coverage is **84.7%**, below nominal 90%, even for the channel-aware method. Therefore this run does not demonstrate calibrated uncertainty under distribution shift. No statistical inference across independent real caves is claimed.

## Related work and novelty limits

SISALv3 is a global stable-isotope and trace-element compilation with data from 365 sites. Frisia's fabric logging provides a method to encode calcite textures into a time series. CaveCalc and other proxy-system models simulate cave chemistry and isotope transfer; Hu et al. compared models against monitored cave data. Bayesian multi-proxy time-series reconstructions are already established. Scholz et al. demonstrated that diagenesis can affect U-series ages, and the Krem Mawmluh fatty-acid study found signatures consistent with both cave and soil sources. Thus fabric logging, diagenesis modeling, multivariate proxy inference, and probabilistic reconstruction are not being claimed as individually novel.

The proposed next contribution is an explicit recoverability/identifiability gate that treats imperfect fabric metadata as side information and evaluates both point error and calibration under held-out caves and channel shift. The literature search is scoped rather than exhaustive. The real-data gate remains open because the necessary co-aligned data fields have not been assembled or tested here.

## Limitations

The simulator does not model isotope fractionation, karst residence-time distributions, mineralogical transformation kinetics, real biomarker chemistry, real age-depth ensembles, or actual cave monitoring. The label mapping and proxy response vectors are arbitrary assumptions. A future real-data study must align fabric labels, chronology, multi-proxy observations, and suitable modern targets, and compare against strong process-based and Bayesian baselines. If those observations do not identify the climate state or intervals fail cave-held-out calibration, the correct conclusion is to restrict or withhold the reconstruction.
