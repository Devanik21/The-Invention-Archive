 An Information-Theoretic Recoverability Framework for Speleothem Climate Archives

                                                Abstract
         Speleothem records preserve valuable evidence about past hydroclimate, but measured
     isotope ratios, trace elements, biomarkers, and chronologies are mediated by karst transport,
     cave processes, biomediation, post-depositional alteration, and measurement error. Existing
     proxy-system models and petrographic workflows address important parts of this forward
     process, while Bayesian multi-proxy reconstructions quantify uncertainty from multiple records.
     This paper develops a complementary, falsifiable framework: treat the archive as a structured
     observation channel and ask whether climate is recoverable from the measured proxies when
     the channel state is only partially observed. The framework combines a linearized multi-proxy
     response model, a latent alteration state, and noisy fabric labels as side information. It
     derives an explicit identifiability condition, a Gaussian information-gain expression, and a fail-
     closed rule for reporting or withholding reconstructions. A reproducible synthetic benchmark
     compares single-proxy, multi-proxy, and fabric-informed linear decoders on held-out synthetic
     cave sequences under matched and shifted alteration. In this deliberately assumed simulator,
     channel-aware decoding modestly lowers RMSE relative to a multi-proxy baseline (0.2686
     versus 0.2739 in-distribution; 0.3007 versus 0.3132 under the specified shift), but nominal 90%
     prediction-interval coverage falls to 84.7% under shift. The experiment therefore supports a
     methodological warning more strongly than a performance claim: side information can help,
     yet distribution shift and label noise can still produce undercoverage. No SISAL records, cave
     monitoring data, or laboratory measurements were analyzed. Because structured fabric labels
     and instrumental target series are not guaranteed to co-occur in the public global compilation,
     empirical validation remains a separate, currently unmet gate. The contribution is a testable
     recoverability framework and reproducible stress test, not a claim of a validated paleoclimate
     decoder.

Keywords: speleothems; proxy-system models; diagenesis; calcite fabrics; information theory;
inverse problems; Bayesian reconstruction; uncertainty calibration; paleoclimate


1    Research posture and contribution
A speleothem is not a tape recorder that maps climate variables directly to measured proxy
values. Rainfall amount and isotope composition are filtered by soil and karst storage; dripwater
chemistry and cave temperature influence precipitation; growth and fabric respond to hydrology,
supersaturation, particles, organic compounds, and biological processes; and post-depositional

                                                     1
DECODING DEEP TIME THROUGH A DIAGENETIC CHANNEL                                                      2


change can alter geochemistry or chronology. This forward complexity is documented in proxy-
system models, fabric logging, and studies of diagenesis [2, 3, 6, 7]. A reconstruction is an inverse
problem: from the remaining observations, infer the climate history and quantify the ambiguity.
   We ask: under what conditions do multiple proxy records plus fabric-side information constrain
climate more than the proxies alone, and when is the information loss too severe for a decoder
to recover it? The answer should not be “use a larger generative model by default.” A flexible
decoder may regularize an ill-posed inverse problem, but it cannot manufacture identifying
information absent from the observations. It may also be confidently wrong when the modern
calibration domain and the deep-time archive differ.

Contribution. This work proposes (i) a structured-channel observation model that separates
climate response from alteration and measurement error; (ii) a response-geometry identifiability
condition; (iii) information and uncertainty quantities conditional on fabric metadata; (iv) a
reproducible synthetic stress test against transparent baselines; and (v) a preregisterable empirical
protocol with explicit failure criteria. The framework formalizes and connects established ideas;
it does not establish that a novel physical diagenetic process exists, nor that an AI decoder has
recovered real paleoclimate.

Novelty posture. Frisia’s systematic microstratigraphic logging already turns fabrics into
coded time series that can help distinguish hydrological, biomediation, and diagenetic influences
[2]. CaveCalc and other proxy-system models already forward-model multiple chemical and
isotope systems through cave processes [6]. Bayesian approaches already combine multiple
paleoclimate archives and return probabilistic reconstructions [1]. Diagenetic alteration and
U-series age effects have long been investigated, including models of uranium gain/loss and age
inversions [7]. The limited claim here is the explicit synthesis of these pieces into a recoverability-
first evaluation rule, paired with a synthetic comparison that tests both error and uncertainty
under a declared channel shift. The literature search is not exhaustive; no absolute priority claim
is made.


2     Evidence base and closest prior work
2.1   What existing records and methods support

SISALv3 is a major global compilation of speleothem stable isotope and trace-element records,
reporting data from 365 sites and providing updated records and associated resources [5].
This scale is important for comparative reconstruction, but a geochemical compilation is not
automatically a supervised machine-learning table: models require compatible observations of
targets, proxy time series, chronologies, fabrics/alteration labels, and cave monitoring variables
at appropriate temporal support. SISALv3’s published description does not guarantee a unified,
per-sample fabric label plus modern instrumental climate target for every record. This is an
explicit data audit requirement before empirical work begins.
   The Krem Mawmluh study of fatty acids reported evidence consistent with compounds arising
from both cave microbial activity and overlying soil sources, and observed covariation with
other biomarkers and isotope proxies; it also called for more analytical reference points [4].

This demonstrates that biological material can carry environmental information and mixed
provenance—not that every biomarker is noise or that microbial effects can be subtracted with
a universal model. Similarly, diagenesis can affect uranium-series chronology and produce age
inversions; its effects depend on alteration history and geochemical conditions [7]. These processes
should be represented as candidate channel states with uncertain mappings, not collapsed into
one undifferentiated error term.

3.1       A structured observation channel

A local linear approximation to a nonlinear proxy-system model is

                                 yt = Axt + Bd zd,t + Bm zm,t + ϵt ,                                   (1)

where zd,t is a diagenetic state and zm,t a microbial/provenance state. With a vector climate
target, Axt generalizes naturally to Axt . Coefficients can vary with cave, temperature, hydrology,
mineralogy, or time; the constant-matrix form is a local approximation, not a universal physical
law.
  Fabric metadata provides side information through a noisy measurement model,

                                         p(ft | zd,t , zm,t , ht ),                                    (2)

where ht contains observed petrographic/stratigraphic covariates. Equation 2 must be calibrated
from real paired samples; without paired labels, a model cannot infer the physical meaning of
a fabric category from its name alone. Fabric can be climate-informative as well as alteration-
informative. Thus it should not automatically be treated as an exclusion flag or as a pure
nuisance proxy.
  A generative decoder seeks the posterior

                                       p(x, zd , zm , a | y, f , D),                                   (3)

where D represents calibration records and priors. A practical model may be hierarchical and
non-Gaussian, but its posterior is only interpretable to the extent that its likelihood, calibration
domain, label process, and chronology model are justified. A diffusion or transformer prior is not
required at the first validation stage: a transparent baseline is preferable until there are enough
correctly aligned paired observations to support richer models.


3.2   Identifiability before decoder scale

Proposition 1 (A sufficient ambiguity condition in a linear channel). Suppose y = Ax + Bz + ϵ,
with no side information on z, and there are nonzero vectors ∆x and ∆z such that A∆x = −B∆z.
Then the noiseless observation is unchanged when (x, z) is replaced by (x +∆x, z +∆z). Therefore
the observation alone cannot uniquely identify both states over a domain containing those
alternatives.

Proof sketch. Substitution gives A(x + ∆x) + B(z + ∆z) = Ax + Bz. The observed vector is
identical. A prior can select between the alternatives but does not create new observational
evidence; conclusions then depend on the prior.                                           □
  This is a P1 algebraic statement conditional on the linear model, not a claim that any
specific real cave has this degeneracy. Near-overlap between the column spaces of climate and
nuisance response matrices produces practical ill-conditioning even if exact non-identifiability is
absent. Diagnostics should report singular values, condition numbers, and sensitivity to plausible
nuisance priors. Additional independent proxies help only if their response directions contain
new information rather than simply more measurements of the same confounded combination.

5     Synthetic validation design
5.1      Purpose and simulator

The first test is intentionally synthetic. It asks whether the proposed modeling distinctions
behave as expected when the data-generating process is known, and exposes failure modes before
real data are used. It does not establish that the synthetic loadings are representative of karst,
calcite, microbes, or a climate regime.
   Climate xt follows an autoregressive process with coefficient ϕ = 0.78. Three proxy channels use
assumed climate loadings (1.00, 0.70, 0.42), while alteration states add structured contributions
with assumed diagenetic loadings (0.86, −0.43, 0.24) and microbial loadings (−0.20, 0.66, −0.78).
Proxy-specific background noise has standard deviation 0.34. The three-state channel follows a
persistent Markov-like sequence; fabric observations misclassify the true synthetic state at an
assumed 10% rate. Parameters are declared in results/synthetic_generation_spec.json.
   Training uses 72 synthetic caves by 170 time points (12,240 records). Two held-out scenarios
each contain 20 synthetic caves by 170 time points (3,400 records): an in-distribution state
mixture and a deliberately shifted alteration prevalence/scale. The split is by synthetic cave, not
by randomly shuffled time points, to avoid an unrealistically easy within-cave leak. The current
script fits ordinary least-squares estimators to: (1) one proxy; (2) all three proxies; (3) the
same proxies plus one-hot observed fabric labels. Predictive intervals use a linear-model residual
variance formula. They are reference intervals, not posterior intervals from a full hierarchical
Bayesian model.

5.2    Prespecified acceptance gates

A useful channel-aware decoder should improve held-out reconstruction error against the multi-
proxy baseline by a practically stated margin, preserve or improve interval calibration, remain
stable across held-out caves, and avoid spuriously narrow intervals under channel shift. For an
empirical study, a proposed first gate is: cave-grouped held-out RMSE improvement of at least
5% over the multi-proxy baseline, nominal 90% interval coverage statistically compatible with
90% on held-out caves, and no substantial worsening of log score. These thresholds are design
choices and should be preregistered before viewing real held-out results. The current synthetic
study is not expected to satisfy all gates by construction; failure is useful.

6.1                 The calibration failure matters

The channel-aware estimator has better point scores in both scenarios, but the shifted-scenario
coverage is 84.7%, below the nominal 90%. The multi-proxy baseline reaches 83.6%, and the
single-proxy baseline collapses to 67.2%. These gaps imply that a future real-data study must
assess predictive intervals at the level of held-out caves and channel regimes. A model that “wins”
by RMSE while delivering systematically overconfident intervals is not yet suitable for historical
uncertainty statements.

Figure 7 plots one synthetic sequence to expose local behavior that aggregate metrics hide. It

single series is not a validation sample. An empirical version should summarize all held-out

7     Sensitivity, competing explanations, and failure modes
A channel-aware improvement has several possible explanations: genuinely useful side information;
leakage through correlated labels; model flexibility; fortuitous loading geometry; or an overly
benign synthetic label process. The released code uses a cave-grouped split, but all train and
test sets are generated from the same fixed simulator family. The shift changes state prevalence
and disturbance amplitude while retaining the assumed response loadings. More severe forms of
shift—changed coefficients, entirely new mineralogy, non-linear proxy saturation, missing-not-
at-random measurements, altered age-depth relations, or unseen fabrics—are not tested in this
experiment.

8     Proposed empirical study and falsification plan
8.1    Data gate before model selection

The minimum viable real-data dataset must align: (1) proxy observations and sample locations;
(2) usable age-depth ensembles; (3) standardized fabric and petrographic alteration labels tied to
those samples; (4) modern cave monitoring or an external target appropriate to the reconstruction
task; and (5) cave-level splits so measurements from one cave cannot appear in both calibration
and test partitions. Before model fitting, create a data availability matrix by record and field. If
paired fabric/alteration labels and instrumental targets do not coexist at adequate scale, do not
quietly impute them from unrelated sites and treat the result as supervised validation. Instead
perform a transparent feasibility study, label-model analysis, and synthetic experiment, and
postpone claims about empirical decoding.
   A realistic path is a two-part design. First, use modern monitored caves with overlapping
rainfall, dripwater, cave temperature, and speleothem proxy data to estimate forward response
distributions and test whether fabrics carry incremental information about channel state. Second,
evaluate the posterior decoder on held-out caves, then on longer archives using age-model
ensembles. The target variable needs to be specific: instrumental rainfall anomaly, rainfall
isotope composition, effective moisture, cave dripwater isotope, or another quantity are not
interchangeable targets.

8.2    Predeclared pass and fail conditions

A real-data study should register train/test cave assignments, comparator models, outcome
metrics, missing-data handling, age uncertainty treatment, and threshold rules before opening
test targets. The proposed primary metric is cave-grouped RMSE versus a strong multi-proxy
baseline, accompanied by empirical coverage, mean interval width, continuous ranked probability
score or log score, and calibration curves. Each cave is an independent grouping unit; thousands
of within-cave time points must not be treated as thousands of independent caves.

   The first falsification result would be a failure to improve predictive accuracy over a calibrated

9     Limitations and responsible interpretation
The current code is a synthetic benchmark whose data-generating process is selected and known
by construction. Its “climate,” “microbial,” “diagenetic,” and “fabric” variables have arbitrary
standardized units and illustrative coefficients. It does not model isotope fractionation, karst
residence-time distributions, calcite/aragonite transformation kinetics, real biomarker chemistry,
U–Th isotopic equations, age-depth ensembles, speleothem growth, or actual cave monitoring.
The three proxies are not calibrated proxies, the three-state fabric variable is not a petrographic
taxonomy, and the reported prediction intervals omit major sources of uncertainty. Numerical
success can only confirm that the implementation behaves consistently under this simulator.
   The framework could be useful for better reconstruction of monsoon and regional hydroclimate
variability if a calibrated forward model and reliable chronology support the inference. It could
also identify archives where a quantitative climate signal cannot be separated from nuisance
processes. But the path from this paper to any such benefit requires paired modern observations,
lab-based mineralogical/geochemical analyses, independent chronology, careful model comparison,
and community scrutiny. No direct human health, climate-policy, or engineering utility is claimed.
    Ethical and epistemic safeguards include preserving original measurements, labeling inferred

and observed values separately, documenting provenance and missingness, making uncertainty
visible, and not replacing expert petrographic assessment with a black-box score. Model outputs
should complement rather than overrule cave-specific geological interpretation.


10     Conclusion
Viewing speleothem reconstruction as a structured inverse channel is useful only if the model can
identify what the archive actually preserves. This paper formalizes climate–alteration ambiguity,
expresses the potential contribution of fabric-side information in a Gaussian baseline, and provides
reproducible synthetic comparisons against single- and multi-proxy estimators. The modest
synthetic accuracy gain and shifted-coverage failure indicate that adding a channel label does not
eliminate uncertainty or guarantee calibration. The next decisive step is not a larger generative
neural network; it is a data audit and held-out-cave validation with co-located fabrics, altered
mineralogy indicators, chronology ensembles, and modern target measurements. Until those
data are aligned and the empirical gates pass, the proposed method remains a falsifiable research
framework rather than a validated paleoclimate reconstruction system.
