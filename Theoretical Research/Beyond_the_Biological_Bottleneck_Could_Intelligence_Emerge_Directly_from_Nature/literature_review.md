# Literature review and evidence map

**Scope:** targeted review for *Beyond the Biological Bottleneck* (30 October 2026). This is not a systematic review, meta-analysis, patent search, or claim of exhaustive coverage. The focus is on physical computation, abiotic chemical organisation, agency, planetary-scale intelligence, thermodynamics of information, and the interpretation of extraterrestrial evidence.

## 1. Chemical and physical computation

**Baltussen et al. (2024), Nature 631, 549–555.** “Chemical reservoir computation in a self-organizing reaction network.” DOI: [10.1038/s41586-024-07567-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07567-x). This is the most direct experimental anchor for the paper. The authors demonstrate a chemical reaction network used as a reservoir-computing substrate, with nonlinear classification and prediction/forecasting tasks. The result supports the proposition that useful computation can be physically implemented in a self-organising chemical medium. It does not show autonomous goal pursuit, general learning, self-maintenance, consciousness, or abiotic intelligence.

**Stepney (2024), Natural Computing 23, 665–685.** “Physical reservoir computing: a tutorial.” DOI: [10.1007/s11047-024-09997-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11047-024-09997-y). A tutorial on mapping inputs into a physical dynamical system and reading out task-relevant transformations. Important distinction: task performance is supplied by a carefully designed input/system/readout architecture; it is not evidence that the substrate independently chose a goal.

## 2. Abiotic chemical organisation and origins of life

**Hordijk & Steel (2018), Life 8(4), 62.** “Autocatalytic networks at the basis of life's origin and organization.” DOI: [10.3390/life8040062](https://doi.org/10.3390/life8040062). Reviews reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated (RAF) networks and their relevance to chemical organisation and the origin of life. Autocatalysis supplies a useful formal language for collectively self-producing reaction networks. It should not be equated with intelligence: closure, catalysis, persistence, and evolution are distinct from general adaptive information processing.

**Barge et al. (2026), PNAS 123(33), e2520594123.** “Emergence of biosignatures on Earth and implications for life detection.” DOI: [10.1073/pnas.2520594123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2520594123). Provides context on the abiotic-to-prebiotic-to-biotic continuum and the possibility that biosignature interpretation is sensitive to evolutionary and geochemical context. Relevance: supports cautious treatment of transitions and false-positive boundaries, not a direct theory of intelligence.

## 3. Agency and planetary-scale systems

**Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde (2009), Adaptive Behavior 17(5), 367–386.** “Defining Agency: Individuality, Normativity, Asymmetry, and Spatio-temporality in Action.” DOI: [10.1177/1059712309343819](https://doi.org/10.1177/1059712309343819). Offers a conceptual analysis of agency involving individuality, normativity, asymmetry, and temporal/spatial organisation. It motivates the manuscript's insistence that stimulus-response complexity alone is insufficient for agency claims.

**Frank, Grinspoon & Walker (2022), International Journal of Astrobiology 21(2), 47–61.** “Intelligence as a planetary scale process.” DOI: [10.1017/S147355042100029X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S147355042100029X). Develops the planetary-intelligence perspective in which technological and planetary systems are coupled. In this paper it is treated as an extension of the scale of the intelligence question, not as proof that a planet is a unitary conscious mind or that planetary feedback is equivalent to agency.

## 4. Biosignature and technosignature inference

**Neveu et al. (2018), Astrobiology 18(11), 1375–1402.** “The Ladder of Life Detection.” DOI: [10.1089/ast.2017.1773](https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2017.1773). Mission-aware framework for structuring life-detection claims and evidence. It informs the staged approach and separation of signal, interpretation, and confidence.

**Cleaves et al. (2023), PNAS 120(41), e2307149120.** “A robust, agnostic molecular biosignature based on machine learning.” DOI: [10.1073/pnas.2307149120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2307149120). Shows how multivariate molecular measurement patterns can support agnostic classification. This is a different modality from active dynamical probing and does not resolve intelligence-origin inference.

**Graham (2026), Annual Review of Earth and Planetary Sciences 54, 585–604.** “Agnostic Biosignatures: Expanding the Search for Life in the Solar System.” DOI: [10.1146/annurev-earth-040722-101044](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-earth-040722-101044). Recent review contextualising approaches that do not assume a narrow Earth-like molecular inventory. Supports the broader methodological motivation while preserving the distinction between life detection and intelligence detection.

**ten Kate et al. (2026), Nature Astronomy 10, 624–629.** “False negatives in the search for extraterrestrial life.” DOI: [10.1038/s41550-026-02863-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-026-02863-0). Highlights how search strategies can miss life and the importance of detection assumptions. Relevant to false-negative control, though not about abiotic intelligence specifically.

**Wright (2021), Acta Astronautica 188, 203–214.** “Strategies and advice for the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence.” DOI: [10.1016/j.actaastro.2021.07.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2021.07.021). Provides methodological context for SETI search strategies, assumptions, and technosignature classes. It informs the distinction between detecting a phenomenon and attributing its origin.

**International Academy of Astronautics SETI Committee (2026).** “Declaration of Principles Concerning the Conduct of the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence” (2026 update, 1 June 2026). [Committee resource](https://iaa-seti-committee.github.io/). Used as a current context for candidate verification, reporting, archiving, and communication discipline; institutional guidance is not empirical support for any abiotic intelligence hypothesis.

## 5. Thermodynamics of information

**Landauer (1961), IBM Journal of Research and Development 5(3), 183–191.** “Irreversibility and heat generation in the computing process.” DOI: [10.1147/rd.53.0183](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.53.0183). Establishes the lower bound associated with logically irreversible bit erasure under ideal assumptions. The manuscript explicitly avoids treating this as a universal energy cost for every computation or an intelligence-specific signature.

**Wimsatt et al. (2021), Journal of Statistical Physics 183, article 16.** “Refining Landauer's stack: balancing error and dissipation when erasing information.” DOI: [10.1007/s10955-021-02733-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-021-02733-1). Refines the relation between error and dissipation, supporting the need to be precise about physical implementation and operating regime.

## 6. Gap and paper positioning

The established literature provides (i) physical media that compute, (ii) abiotic reaction networks with self-organising or autocatalytic structure, (iii) theories of agency and planetary-scale coupling, and (iv) methods for interpreting biosignatures and technosignatures under uncertainty. These areas do not automatically answer the question posed here.

The focused contribution is a **two-axis origin-and-capability framework**. A system's functional capacity (computation, adaptation, prediction, control) is separated from its causal history (direct abiotic emergence, construction by an earlier intelligence, or non-intelligent natural mimic). A simple likelihood-equivalence proposition identifies a structural limit: if all available functional observations have the same distribution under direct emergence and construction, no classifier can update their relative odds from those observations alone. Additional evidence must be genuinely origin-sensitive and independently validated.

This is a conceptual and methodological contribution, not a proof that direct abiotic intelligence exists, not a comprehensive taxonomy of alien minds, and not a priority claim. The synthetic data are designed to demonstrate the conditional logic only.
